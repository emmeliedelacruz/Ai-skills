# Demand-Gen Clip Selection

Use this reference when reviewing a timestamped transcript.

## Scoring rubric

Score each candidate 0–2 on each dimension:

1. **Hook** — does the first sentence stop attention without setup?
2. **Pain / tension** — does it name a problem, risk, desire, or misconception?
3. **Point of view** — is there a distinct opinion, reframe, or surprising observation?
4. **Buyer relevance** — would the intended audience care immediately?
5. **Payoff** — does the clip finish the thought cleanly?
6. **Specificity / proof** — example, number, contrast, mechanism, or concrete advice?
7. **Demand creation** — does it increase desire for a solution/category/product?
8. **Standalone clarity** — can a cold viewer understand it without the full source?

Strong candidate: 12+ / 16.
Exceptional candidate: 14+ / 16.

Do not surface a mediocre clip simply to fill a quota.

## Duration

- Sweet spot: 15–35 seconds
- Acceptable: under 15 seconds when the idea is unusually sharp
- Hard max: 45 seconds unless the user changes the rule

If the transcript section is longer than 45 seconds, look for a tighter in-point or out-point rather than truncating the payoff.

## Hook hierarchy

Prefer, in roughly this order:
- strong contrarian statement
- clear pain / mistake
- specific outcome or result
- surprising fact or observation
- direct "here's what to do" instruction
- concise product/category insight

Avoid opening with:
- "so..."
- "as I mentioned earlier..."
- speaker introductions
- housekeeping
- a question whose answer depends on prior context

## Demand-gen vs. educational-only

Educational content can still create demand, but the clip should do at least one of these:
- make the current way feel costly, slow, risky, or incomplete
- make a better future state feel concrete
- show why the audience's existing assumption is wrong
- give the audience language for a problem they already feel
- reveal a mechanism that makes the solution category more valuable
- demonstrate expertise in a way that earns future consideration

## Suggested headline rule

Write the headline after selecting the clip.

Headline should be:
- shorter than the spoken explanation
- one idea only
- usually 3–9 words
- understandable on mute
- not clickbait that the clip fails to deliver

Examples of headline shapes:
- "People buy solutions, not software."
- "The trap most builders fall into"
- "Your waitlist is a positioning test"
- "Don't validate with friends and family"
